The Problem

AI shouldn't be another tab.

Your employees already have ChatGPT. Maybe Claude, maybe Copilot, maybe six other tools someone expensed last quarter.

They're still doing this:

Answering the same customer questions from scratch, one email at a time. Copying call notes into the CRM, then into a proposal, then into a follow-up. Building reports out of 15 open browser tabs. Chasing coworkers for updates buried in someone else's inbox. Moving information between systems by hand, all day.

The tools changed. The work didn't. That's the gap we close.

Our Approach

Most companies start with the tool. We start with the leak.

"Which platform?" "Which model?" "Should we build an agent?" Our first question is different: where is your business losing the most time, money, or capacity right now?

Find that workflow. Map how it really runs, not how the SOP says it runs. Then rebuild it so people, AI, automation, and your software each handle the part they're best at.

Skip the six-month transformation program. Start with one win your CFO can see. Get that one right and AI stops being an experiment. It becomes part of how the company runs.

Case Study

Noosa Pest Management

Customer emails took 10 to 15 minutes each. Now they take under 2.

<2min Response time
down from 10–15 min
20–25hrs Team capacity created per week(estimated)
6 Specialized AI agents in production
$176.8K ARR protected via faster emergency response(estimated)

Noosa's team handled 20 to 30 customer emails a day by hand. Each one meant reading the request, identifying the problem, finding the right information, writing a response, and making sure urgent cases didn't sit in the queue.

We rebuilt the workflow around six specialized AI agents. One sorts each email and sets urgency. One reads the customer's tone and recommends how to respond. One pulls company policies, services, pricing, and service areas. One checks pest identification and technician availability. One drafts the response in Noosa's voice. One checks the final draft for accuracy and tone before a person sees it.

A team member reviews the response, edits if necessary, and sends.

The technology isn't the point. The point is that work that previously took 10 to 15 minutes can now be reviewed and completed in under 2.

The Chief AI Ops Method™

We begin with the work. The technology comes later.

1
Find it
Identify the workflow with the biggest financial or operational return. Not simply something AI could automate.
2
Redesign it
Map the workflow and decide what humans, AI, automation, and your systems each handle. Humans keep judgment. AI handles research and drafting. Automation handles routing and logging.
3
Build and ship it
Agents, knowledge base, integrations, human approval points, testing, deployment, documentation, and training. Into actual production.
4
Prove it
Measure the baseline. Measure the new workflow. Compare the two. If we can't show where the money comes from, we don't automate the workflow.

The first workflow becomes the blueprint for the next one.

Nobody wants a six-agent RAG workflow.

You want customer inquiries answered before the customer calls a competitor. Salespeople selling. Reports finished faster, with a human still checking the sources.

The agents are how we get you there. You're paying for the result.

The machine does the machine work.

The best AI workflows aren't fully autonomous. They shouldn't be. AI handles repetitive research, drafting, classification, routing, and information processing. Your people keep the judgment, relationships, accountability, and final call when the stakes are high.

The machine does more machine work. Your people do more of the work you hired people to do.
