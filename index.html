The Problem

AI shouldn't be another tab.

Your employees already have ChatGPT. Maybe Claude, maybe Copilot, maybe six other tools someone expensed last quarter.

They're still doing this:

Answering the same customer questions from scratch, one email at a time. Copying call notes into the CRM, then into a proposal, then into a follow-up. Building reports out of 15 open browser tabs. Chasing coworkers for updates buried in someone else's inbox. Moving information between systems by hand, all day.

The tools changed. The work didn't. That's the gap we close.

Our Approach

Most companies start with the tool. We start with the leak.

Find that workflow. Map how it really runs, not how the SOP says it runs. Then rebuild it so people, AI, automation, and your software each handle the part they're best at.

Skip the six-month transformation program. Start with one win your CFO can see. A workflow your team touches every day. One management already knows is slow. Where the math is obvious. Get that one right and AI stops being an experiment. It becomes part of how the company runs.

What a Rebuilt Workflow Looks Like

What a rebuilt workflow looks like

Customer Service

Triage and drafting done before anyone opens the email

Before

An employee reads the email, figures out the category, searches for the answer, writes a reply, rereads it, and sends. At Noosa Pest Management, that took 10 to 15 minutes per email.

After

AI classifies the message, pulls the right company information, and drafts the reply. Unusual or high-risk cases go straight to a person. At Noosa, a team member now reviews and sends in under 2 minutes.

The change: Your team edits drafts instead of starting from scratch.

Sales

The call ends and the CRM is already updated

Before

Notes sit in a notebook until someone remembers the CRM. The rep writes the follow-up, digs up materials, and assembles the proposal by hand.

After

The call is captured. The CRM updates itself. The follow-up is drafted. Account research is attached. The proposal comes together from approved company language. The rep reviews and personalizes.

The change: Salespeople get their afternoons back for selling.

Research & Reporting

The first draft builds itself

Before

Fifteen browser tabs. Copy, paste, format, check, repeat.

After

AI handles the gathering, first-pass compilation, and formatting. Your analyst reviews the evidence and makes the call.

The change: Your team does the thinking. AI does the gathering.

Operations

People stop acting as middleware

Before

A request arrives, gets forwarded, waits for a reply, gets retyped into another system, confirmed, and logged by hand.

After

Requests route themselves. Approvals trigger. Systems update. Confirmations go out. Logs write themselves. People only see the exceptions.

The change: Less routing. More deciding.

The Chief AI Ops Method™

We begin with the work. The technology comes later.

1
Find it
Identify the workflow with the biggest financial or operational return. Not just the one AI could touch.
2
Redesign it
Map the current process, then hand each step to whoever does it best. Humans keep judgment and accountability. AI takes research and drafting. Automation handles routing and logging. Your existing systems store and move the data.
3
Build and ship it
AI agents, knowledge base, integrations, human approval checkpoints, testing, deployment, and training.
4
Prove it
We record the baseline before we build, then measure the change. If we can't show where the money comes from, we don't automate the workflow.

The first workflow becomes the blueprint for the next one.

Case Study

Noosa Pest Management

Customer emails took 10 to 15 minutes each. Now they take under 2.

<2min Response time
down from 10–15 min
20–25hrs Team capacity created per week(estimated)
6 Specialized AI agents in production
$176.8K ARR protected via faster emergency response(estimated)

Noosa's team handled 20 to 30 customer emails a day by hand. Every one meant reading the request, figuring out the problem, finding the right information, writing a reply, and making sure urgent cases didn't sit in the queue.

The rebuilt workflow splits the job across six agents. One sorts each email and sets urgency. One reads the customer's tone and recommends how to respond. One pulls company policies, services, pricing, and service areas. One checks pest identification and technician availability. One drafts the reply in Noosa's voice. One checks the draft for accuracy and tone before a person ever sees it.

A team member reviews the draft, edits if needed, and sends.
